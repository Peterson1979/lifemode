Cleaning and seasoning a cast‑iron skillet isn’t a guess‑work project; it’s a repeatable protocol. Start by scrubbing the surface with hot water and a stiff brush, dry thoroughly, then coat with a thin layer of high‑smoke‑point oil. Heat the skillet in a 450 °F oven for an hour so the oil polymerizes into a durable, non‑stick layer. Repeat the oil‑coat and bake cycle three times for a resilient finish.

## Quick summary & key parameters
- **Temperature:** 450 °F (232 °C) for polymerization; avoid exceeding 500 °F to prevent smoking.
- **Oil choice:** Flaxseed, grapeseed, or refined avocado oil; smoke point ≥ 420 °F.
- **Tools & materials:** Stiff non‑metal brush or scrub pad, clean kitchen towel, sheet pan, oven‑safe gloves.
- **Prep time:** 10 min cleaning, 5 min oiling, 60 min heating per cycle.
- **Safety thresholds:** Keep kitchen well‑ventilated; never leave the oven unattended during the heating cycle.

## Step‑by‑step execution protocol
1. **Pre‑heat the oven** to 450 °F.
2. **Remove rust or residue** – use hot water and a stiff brush; avoid soap unless the skillet is brand‑new.
3. **Dry instantly** – towel dry, then place the pan on a low burner for 2 min to evaporate moisture.
4. **Apply oil** – pour a teaspoon of chosen oil onto the surface, spread with a paper towel until the skillet looks glossy but not wet.
5. **Wipe excess** – a clean towel should leave a barely visible film; excess oil creates sticky spots.
6. **Bake** – place the skillet upside‑down on a sheet pan, bake for 60 min. Allow the oven to cool naturally.
7. **Repeat** – perform steps 4‑6 two more times for a robust polymerized layer.
8. **Cool and store** – once cooled, store the skillet in a dry cabinet; a thin paper towel inside prevents moisture buildup.

**Execution tips:**
- Use a timer for each heating phase.
- Position the skillet’s handle away from the oven wall to avoid heat damage.
- If smoke appears, lower the temperature by 10 °F and increase ventilation.

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Too much oil** – results in a gummy, uneven coating. Fix by stripping the surface with coarse salt and a scrub pad, then restart the protocol.
- **Insufficient heat** – polymerization won’t occur, leaving a slick surface that food sticks to. Verify oven temperature with an independent probe.
- **Skipping drying** – residual water causes oil to pool and rust. Always ensure the skillet is bone‑dry before oiling.
- **Using low‑smoke‑point oil** – the oil will burn, leaving a black residue. Replace with a high‑smoke‑point oil and repeat the bake.

**Pro tips for longevity:**
- After each cooking session, wipe the interior with a thin oil layer while still warm.
- Avoid acidic foods (tomatoes, vinegar) for the first three seasoning cycles.
- Re‑season annually or when the surface looks dull.

Maintaining a cast‑iron skillet with the oil polymerization protocol turns a simple pan into a kitchen workhorse that improves with every use.
