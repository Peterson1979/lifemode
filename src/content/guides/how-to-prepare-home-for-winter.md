## The Physics of Freezing Water in Residential Pipes

Water is one of the few substances that **expands when transitioning from liquid to solid crystalline ice**:

$$\text{Water Volume at 4°C} \xrightarrow{\text{Freezing (0°C)}} \text{Ice Volume (+9.05% Expansion)}$$

Inside a sealed copper or PEX pipe, expanding ice exerts up to **2,000 bar (nearly 30,000 psi)** of hydrostatic pressure against the pipe walls—far exceeding the burst rating of any residential plumbing material.

Proper insulation, exterior drainage, and indoor heating circulation protect against freeze burst damage.
