In modern remote and hybrid work environments, our attention is constantly fragmented.

Studies from cognitive researchers at the University of California, Irvine reveal that it takes an average of **23 minutes and 15 seconds** to regain deep mental focus after a single minor digital interruption (such as checking a Slack message or glancing at a push notification).

If you are interrupted three or four times an hour, your brain never reaches the high-leverage state of deep cognitive flow.

The solution is not working 12-hour marathon days. It is organizing your day around **Ultradian Focus Cycles**—structured 90-minute blocks of unbroken, single-task work separated by intentional cognitive rest.

## How to Structure a 90-Minute Focus Session

Executing a flawless deep work session requires a clear ritual:

1. **The 2-Minute Definition (Min 0–2):** Write down the exact single deliverable on an index card (e.g., *"Draft sections 1 and 2 of client technical report"*).
2. **The Ramp-Up (Min 2–15):** Expect friction in the first 10 minutes. Your brain will want to check email. Resist the urge; flow state naturally engages around minute 15.
3. **Deep Execution (Min 15–75):** Unbroken creative or analytical problem solving.
4. **The Wind-Down &amp; Save (Min 75–90):** Review progress, save clean notes, and write down the exact starting point for your next session.
5. **The True Disconnect (15 Min Break):** Step away from all screens. Walk around the block, stretch, or drink water. Never "rest" by scrolling social media, which causes further cognitive depletion.

Protecting just two 90-minute blocks each day allows you to produce exceptional creative and analytical work while finishing your workday with energy to spare.
