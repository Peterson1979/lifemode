## The practical answer

Put the top of the screen roughly at eye level, keep it about an arm’s length away, and move it until you are not leaning forward. Your elbows should stay near your sides, with the keyboard and mouse close enough that shoulders can relax. Adjust chair height so feet are supported; use a footrest if needed.

Reduce glare by turning the desk away from a bright window, closing blinds, or adding soft room light behind or beside the screen. Increase text size before hunching toward a small display. The 20-20-20 rule—look about 20 feet away for 20 seconds every 20 minutes—is an easy reminder to change focus.

No chair, monitor, or keyboard can replace movement. Stand, walk, and change position through the day. Persistent numbness, weakness, headaches, or eye pain deserves medical or optometric advice rather than another accessory.


## The Biomechanical Reality of the Modern Desk Worker

Sedentary knowledge work places unique physiological demands on human anatomy. The human musculoskeletal system did not evolve to sit motionless for eight hours with forward-tilted cervical vertebrae, wrists pronated flat against a plastic surface, and ciliary eye muscles contracted in static focal lock on a high-luminance display.

Chronic repetitive strain injuries (RSI), thoracic outlet syndrome, cervical disc compression, and digital eye strain are rarely random occurrences. They are the direct mechanical consequence of **poorly calibrated workspace geometry**.

## 1. Display Geometry & The Optometric 20-20-20 Rule

### Monitor Height & Tilt
The natural resting gaze of the human eye is angled approximately **15 to 20 degrees downward** from horizontal eye level.
- **Top Edge Alignment:** The top horizontal bezel of your primary monitor should align directly with your eye line when sitting upright. Looking *up* at a display forces continuous contraction of the suboccipital neck muscles.
- **Focal Distance:** Position the screen approximately **50 to 75 cm (20 to 30 inches)** away—roughly an arm's length. If you must lean forward to read text, increase operating system display scaling (e.g., 125% or 150%) rather than moving the screen closer.

### Visual Rest & Ciliary Relaxation
The internal ciliary muscles of the eye contract to maintain near-field focus. Continuous contraction induces asthenopia (eye strain). Practice the **20-20-20 Rule**: every 20 minutes, gaze at an object at least 20 feet (6 meters) away for 20 continuous seconds to allow the ciliary muscles to fully relax.

## 2. Input Peripherals & Forearm Biomechanics

Traditional flat rectangular keyboards force human forearms into two unnatural postural distortions:
1. **Pronation:** Palms forced flat parallel to the desk, twisting the radius and ulna bones against each other.
2. **Ulnar Deviation:** Wrists bent outward toward the pinky fingers to align with a straight key grid, compressing the median nerve inside the carpal tunnel.

### The Split Ergonomic Solution
- **Split Mechanical Keyboards:** Separating the keyboard into two independent halves allows shoulders to rest in an open, relaxed posture aligned with natural shoulder width.
- **Tenting (5° to 15°):** Angling the inner edges of the keyboard halves upward maintains the forearm in a neutral "handshake" angle, relieving pressure on the median nerve.
- **Vertical Ergonomic Mice:** Tilting the hand at a 57-degree angle neutralizes wrist pronation and shifts movement from small wrist tendons to larger upper-arm muscle groups.

## 3. Desk & Chair Height Alignment

| Anatomical Point | Neutral Geometric Target | Common Misalignment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Feet** | Resting completely flat on floor or angled footrest. | Dangling legs compress femoral arteries on chair edge. |
| **Knees** | 90° to 100° angle with 5 cm clearance behind knee crease. | Seat pan too deep compresses popliteal lymph and nerve clusters. |
| **Elbows** | 90° to 105° resting angle with forearm parallel to floor. | Desk too high forces trapezius muscles to hunch shoulders. |
| **Lumbar Spine** | Supported in gentle natural lordotic curve. | Slouching flattens lumbar spine, multiplying intervertebral disc pressure. |

## 4. Ambient Lighting & Circadian Calibration

- **Eliminate Screen Glare:** Never position monitors directly opposite an unshaded window where exterior sunlight produces high-contrast glare.
- **Asymmetric Screen Bars:** Use a high-CRI (> 95 Color Rendering Index) monitor light bar that projects warm, glare-free illumination directly onto your desk surface without bouncing photons off the screen panel.
- **Warm Evening Shift:** Transition workspace illumination from 5000K (bright daylight white) at noon down to 2700K (warm amber) after sundown to protect circadian melatonin production.
