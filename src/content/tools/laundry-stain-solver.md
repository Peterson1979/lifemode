## How Household Stain Chemistry Works

Stains are divided into four primary biochemical categories, each requiring a specific temperature and active chemical counter-agent:

1. **Tannin & Plant Pigments (Coffee, Tea, Wine, Berries):** Rich in polyphenols and anthocyanins. These require acidic solubilization (distilled white vinegar) or mild oxidizing bleaches (3% hydrogen peroxide). *Never apply hot water first.*
2. **Lipids & Grease (Cooking Oil, Butter, Motor Grease):** Non-polar hydrocarbon chains that repel water. These require concentrated amphiphilic surfactants (dish soap) or mineral absorbents (baking soda, cornstarch) combined with warm water to melt fatty acids.
3. **Proteins (Blood, Egg, Dairy, Sweat):** Complex amino acid chains. Hot water instantly coagulates and bakes proteins into cellulose fibers. Always flush with cold water and treat with protease enzyme detergents.
4. **Solvent Carriers (Ballpoint Ink, Permanent Marker):** Synthetic polymers suspended in glycol or oil solvents. Polar water cannot dissolve them; isopropyl alcohol (70-90%) breaks the binder matrix.
