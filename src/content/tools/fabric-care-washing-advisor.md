## The Science of Textile Washing Chemistry

Caring for fine garments is a matter of organic chemistry and material physics:

1. **Protein Fibers (Wool, Cashmere, Silk):** Composed of keratin and fibroin amino acids. Protease enzymes found in standard commercial detergents break down these peptide bonds, causing microscopic thinning and fabric holes. Always use pH-neutral, enzyme-free detergents.
2. **Cellulose Bast Fibers (Linen, Hemp, Cotton):** Hollow natural plant fibers that thrive in cool to warm water. Over-drying in hot mechanical dryers dehydrates the cell structure, causing fiber brittleness and permanent shrinkage.
3. **Synthetic Polymers (Polyester, Nylon, Spandex):** Hydrophobic petroleum filaments. Liquid fabric softeners deposit silicone coatings that trap odor-causing bacterial lipids inside the weave; avoid softeners and air dry to protect elastane stretch recovery.
