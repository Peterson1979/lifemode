## The practical answer

Calculate profit per order before celebrating revenue. Include product cost, inbound freight, packaging, payment fees, marketplace fees, fulfilment, shipping subsidy, returns, damaged stock, advertising, tax, and your time. Contribution margin is what remains after costs that rise with each sale.

Order a small test batch and track sell-through, repeat demand, returns, and support questions. Set a reorder point using realistic supplier and shipping times, but do not tie up all your cash in inventory. Negotiate quality checks and written specifications before scaling. A 4x markup is not a universal rule; the customer, category, and costs decide whether the price works.

Use one source of truth for stock, reconcile it with the store, and tell customers honestly when a product is backordered. If the numbers only work with perfect conversion or no returns, the business is not ready for a larger purchase.


## The Illusion of Topline E-Commerce Revenue

The e-commerce landscape is littered with brands generating $100,000 per month in gross merchandise value (GMV) while quietly bleeding net cash. In physical product businesses, high platform advertising costs, payment processing fees, shipping surcharges, returns, and inventory holding costs rapidly erode nominal margins.

Operating a sustainable, niche physical products business requires strict adherence to **Contribution Margin 2 (CM2)**: the net cash remaining after all cost of goods sold, freight, pick-and-pack fulfillment, merchant fees, customer acquisition ad spend, and return allowances are accounted for.

## Deconstructing True Landed Cost per Unit

Never rely solely on your factory supplier's invoice price. The true **Fully Landed Cost (FLC)** includes every friction point between manufacturing and your fulfillment center:

$$\text{FLC} = \text{Ex-Works Unit Cost} + \text{Ocean/Air Freight Share} + \text{Customs Duties \& Tariffs} + \text{Port Drayage} + \text{Inbound Receiving Fees}$$

### The Rule of 4x Markup
To absorb blended customer acquisition costs (CAC) and retain a healthy 20–25% net pre-tax margin:
- If a product's Fully Landed Cost is **$15.00**...
- Your minimum retail selling price (MSRP) should target **$60.00** (a 4x markup / 75% gross margin).
- Products with less than a 3x multiplier rarely survive modern digital advertising costs unless supported by massive repeat purchase replenishment cycles.

## Inventory Management: Balancing Stockouts and Cash Drag

Inventory is dead cash until it sells. Two core metrics govern healthy supply chain health:

### 1. Inventory Turnover Ratio (ITR)
Measures how many times inventory is sold and replaced over a 12-month period:

$$\text{ITR} = \frac{\text{Annual Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)}}{\text{Average Inventory Value at Cost}}$$

- **Target Benchmark:** An ITR between **4.0 and 6.0** indicates brisk sales velocity without excessive capital lockup. An ITR below 2.5 warns of stagnant stock and impending obsolescence.

### 2. Days of Inventory Outstanding (DIO)
How many days of demand your current warehouse inventory covers. For overseas manufacturing cycles with a 60-day production-plus-shipping lead time, maintain **75 to 90 Days of Inventory Outstanding** to absorb seasonal customs delays without experiencing catastrophic out-of-stock events.

## Fulfillment Strategy: When to Transition to a 3PL

- **Phase 1: Founder Fulfillment (0 to 200 orders/mo):** Pack orders in-house using desktop thermal label printers. This develops intimate familiarity with packaging integrity, unboxing experience, and shipping carrier rules.
- **Phase 2: Hybrid Micro-3PL (200 to 1,000 orders/mo):** Transition to a regional third-party logistics partner with transparent Pick-Pack-Ship fees ($3.50 – $4.50 base per order plus weight).
- **Phase 3: Multi-Node Distributed Fulfillment (1,000+ orders/mo):** Split stock across East Coast and West Coast warehouses to qualify for 2-day ground shipping zones, reducing carrier zone surcharges.
