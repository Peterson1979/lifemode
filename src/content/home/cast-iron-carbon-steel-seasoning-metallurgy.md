## The practical answer

Seasoning is a thin layer of baked-on oil, not a thick coating of grease. Wash a new pan, dry it completely, wipe on a very thin film of neutral cooking oil, then heat it according to the maker’s instructions. Several thin layers work better than one wet layer.

After cooking, scrape out food, wash with hot water and a little dish soap, dry on the hob, and wipe on a drop of oil. Acidic dishes can weaken a young seasoning layer, so use stainless steel or enamel for long tomato-based simmers. Do not store the pan damp.

If the surface is sticky, use less oil and heat it longer. For rust, scrub back to sound metal, dry thoroughly, and reseason. A little uneven colour is normal; a smooth, dry cooking surface matters more than a black, glossy appearance.


## The Science of Seasoning: A Polymer Matrix, Not Oil

Many home cooks mistakenly believe that seasoning a cast iron or carbon steel skillet simply coats the metal in a layer of cooking oil. In reality, raw oil remains sticky, rancidifies quickly, and washes away with water.

True seasoning is the result of **chemical polymerization and carbonization**. When unsaturated triglycerides are exposed to high heat (above their smoke point) in the presence of atmospheric oxygen and a catalytic iron surface, two chemical reactions occur:

1. **Free Radical Oxidation:** Double bonds in unsaturated fatty acids split and react with oxygen.
2. **Thermal Polymerization:** Monomer lipid molecules link into long, complex three-dimensional hydrocarbon chains, forming a tough, insoluble plastic-like polymer that chemically bonds within the microscopic pores of the iron crystalline structure.

## Lipid Selection: Why Unsaturated Fats Create Superior Seasoning

The ability of an oil to polymerize depends directly on its **iodine value** (the degree of unsaturation). The more carbon-carbon double bonds present, the more reactive cross-linking sites exist:

| Oil Type | Polyunsaturated Fat % | Smoke Point | Polymerization Performance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Grapeseed Oil** | ~70% | 215°C (420°F) | **Gold Standard:** Exceptional hardness, smooth finish, widely available. |
| **Flaxseed Oil (Food-Grade)** | ~72% | 107°C (225°F) | Forms very hard layers, but prone to micro-flaking if applied too thickly. |
| **Refined Avocado Oil** | ~13% (High Monounsaturated) | 270°C (520°F) | High smoke point, but fewer reactive double bonds; slower polymer buildup. |
| **Animal Lard / Butter** | < 10% (High Saturated) | 190°C (375°F) | Saturated fats lack sufficient double bonds; produces softer, less durable glaze. |

## The Definitive 4-Step Oven Seasoning Protocol

1. **Scrub and Strip Bare:** Wash pan with hot water, mild detergent, and steel wool to remove any manufacturing transit wax or degraded surface grease.
2. **Micro-Thin Application:** Warm the pan in an oven at 95°C (200°F) for 15 minutes to expand metal pores. Apply half a teaspoon of grapeseed oil across the entire pan, then use clean cotton towels to buff it until the pan looks completely dry. *If the pan looks wet or glossy, there is too much oil, which will result in tacky, uneven spotting.*
3. **Thermal Cure:** Place upside down in an oven preheated to **230°C (450°F)** for 60 minutes. Turn off the heat and allow the pan to cool completely inside the closed oven.
4. **Repeat:** 3 to 4 micro-thin cycles build a durable foundation. Subsequent cooking (especially searing meats and shallow frying) continues to replenish the polymer matrix naturally.

## The Leidenfrost Phenomenon: Non-Stick Thermal Management

Stainless steel and freshly seasoned carbon steel pans achieve non-stick performance when heated to the **Leidenfrost point** (typically between 190°C and 215°C / 375°F–420°F).

When a few drops of water are flicked onto the preheated surface:
- If the water hisses and evaporates instantly, the pan is too cool (around 100°C); food will bond to the metal pores.
- If the water beads into a single dancing mercury-like sphere that glides across the pan without evaporating, the Leidenfrost threshold is reached. A microscopic vapor cushion supports the food, preventing physical contact with the metal and enabling effortless egg and fish releases.
