## The practical answer

Walk the outside of the home after rain. Look for blocked gutters, overflowing downpipes, damaged flashing, cracked sealant, ponding water, and soil touching cladding. Inside, check ceilings below bathrooms, window corners, under sinks, and the base of exterior walls for staining, soft materials, mould, or musty smells.

Use your hand or a thin strip of tissue around doors and windows to find moving air. Replace worn weatherstripping, but do not seal over a ventilation opening or hide a leak. Run bathroom fans during a shower and for a while afterward; make sure they exhaust outdoors.

Photograph changes and note dates. A small stain that grows, a wet electrical area, structural movement, or persistent mould deserves prompt professional assessment. Do not open a wall or disturb suspected asbestos just to investigate. Prevention is a short seasonal walk, clean gutters, safe ventilation, and quick action when water appears.


## Building Envelope Science: The Four Control Layers

A house is a dynamic environmental separator. The building envelope (composed of roof, walls, windows, and foundation) must balance four critical physical transport mechanisms in order of structural risk:

1. **Bulk Liquid Water Control (Highest Risk):** Rain, groundwater, and surface runoff. Liquid water creates rapid wood rot, structural foundation settling, and catastrophic toxic mold outbreaks.
2. **Air Leakage Control:** Unconditioned air traveling through unsealed wall penetrations, electrical outlets, and attic bypasses, carrying moisture vapor with it.
3. **Vapor Diffusion Control:** Microscopic water molecules slowly diffusing through porous drywall and masonry.
4. **Thermal Control:** Insulation resisting heat conduction across seasonal temperature gradients.

## The Physics of Condensation & The Dew Point Trap

Air's capacity to hold moisture is governed by temperature: warm air holds significantly more water vapor than cold air. When humid indoor air leaks into an exterior wall cavity during winter and contacts cold exterior sheathing, its temperature drops to the **dew point**—the temperature at which relative humidity reaches 100%.

The vapor condenses into liquid water inside the wall assembly, unseen by occupants, leading to hidden structural rot and insulation compaction.

## The 4-Zone Seasonal Home Envelope Audit

### Zone 1: Roof Drainage & Foundation Grade
- **Downspout Discharge:** Verify downspouts extend at least **1.5 to 2 meters (5 to 6 feet)** away from foundation walls. Splash blocks that dump water adjacent to basement footers cause hydrostatic pressure cracks.
- **Slope Grade:** Soil surrounding the perimeter foundation must slope away at a minimum drop of 1 inch per foot for the first 6 feet.

### Zone 2: Attic Air Sealing & Insulation Depth
- **Attic Hatch Gasket:** Inspect weatherstripping around the attic access panel; unsealed hatches act as continuous convective chimneys pulling conditioned air into the unheated attic.
- **Soffit Vent Clearance:** Ensure blown cellulose or fiberglass insulation does not block soffit vents. Continuous airflow from soffits to ridge vents prevents hot roof spots that form destructive winter ice dams.

### Zone 3: Mechanical HVAC & MERV Calibration
- **Filter Sizing & Static Pressure:** High MERV ratings trap smaller particulates, but excessively dense filters (MERV 13+) can induce excessive static pressure drop across residential blower motors, reducing airflow and causing evaporator coil freeze-ups. For most residential systems without ECM variable-speed blowers, **MERV 8 to 11** provides optimal filtration without mechanical strain.
- **Condensate Drain Line:** Flush the A/C condensate PVC line annually with distilled white vinegar to eliminate algae slime plugs that trigger ceiling overflow backups.

### Zone 4: Crawlspace & Basement Relative Humidity
- Maintain subterranean relative humidity strictly **below 50%** year-round using a dedicated commercial dehumidifier. Relative humidity exceeding 60% provides the threshold required for mold spore germination (*Aspergillus* and *Penicillium*) and subterranean wood-boring beetle colonization.
