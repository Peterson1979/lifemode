## The short answer

For home use, USDA FSIS says either wood or a nonporous surface such as plastic, marble, glass, or pyroceramic can be used. The key control is separation: if possible, reserve one board for fresh produce and bread and another for raw meat, poultry, and seafood. Wash the board with hot, soapy water after each use, rinse it, and let it air-dry or pat it dry with a clean paper towel.

Material affects care, but no material makes cross-contamination impossible. Choose a board that you can keep clean and replace it when wear creates grooves that are difficult to clean.

## Wood versus nonporous surfaces

| Choice | Useful when | Care boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wood | You want a solid board and are willing to wash and dry it carefully | Follow its care instructions; do not assume every wood board is dishwasher-safe |
| Plastic or another nonporous surface | You want a surface that is generally easier to clean and may be dishwasher-safe | Confirm dishwasher suitability; replace it when grooves become hard to clean |
| Glass, marble, or pyroceramic | You prefer a nonporous surface | Check the manufacturer's care guidance |

USDA FSIS does not make “wood” or “plastic” the universal answer for home consumers. The practical decision is whether the board's surface, care instructions, condition, and separation system fit your kitchen.

## A safe care routine

### 1. Separate before you start

Use a dedicated raw-protein board when possible. Keep raw meat, poultry, and seafood away from produce and other foods that will be eaten without further cooking. If you have only one board, plan the order so ready-to-eat foods are prepared first, then wash the board before it is used for raw foods.

### 2. Wash between food categories and after use

Wash the board, knife, and nearby food-contact surfaces with hot, soapy water after preparing each food item and before moving to the next food category. Rinse and air-dry or pat dry with a clean paper towel.

### 3. Sanitize only with the documented food-contact method

USDA FSIS describes a sanitizing solution of 1 tablespoon of unscented liquid chlorine bleach per gallon of water for cutting boards after cleaning, followed by rinsing and drying. Use this only as directed by the source and the product label; never mix bleach with other cleaners.

### 4. Use the dishwasher only when the board allows it

Nonporous acrylic, plastic, or glass boards and some solid wood boards may be dishwasher-safe; laminated boards may crack or split. The board's own instructions control. If there is doubt, hand-wash it.

### 5. Replace a worn board

Discard a plastic or wooden board when it is excessively worn or has grooves, cracks, or damage that are hard to clean. Do not try to preserve a surface that cannot be cleaned effectively.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating material as a substitute for separation:** A wood or plastic board can still transfer contamination if it is shared without cleaning.
- **Cleaning only at the end of cooking:** Wash between food categories, especially before switching from raw protein to ready-to-eat food.
- **Using a cleaner not approved for food-contact surfaces:** Follow the label and use a food-contact-safe sanitizer after cleaning when sanitizing is needed.
- **Keeping a deeply scored board:** Replace it when grooves or cracks are difficult to clean.

## FAQs

### Should I buy two boards?

It is a practical way to separate raw-protein preparation from produce and bread. If you have one board, clean it between food categories and plan the preparation order carefully.

### Can I use a bamboo board?

USDA FSIS describes bamboo as harder and less porous than hardwoods and recommends hot, soapy water; it also says to sanitize if desired and use mineral oil to help retain moisture. Follow the board's care instructions and still use separation and cleaning practices.

### Does washing remove every food-safety risk?

Cleaning is one control, not a guarantee. Keep raw foods separate, wash hands and nearby utensils, use safe food-handling practices, and replace boards that cannot be cleaned effectively.
