## The short answer

Start with two measurements: how large the space is and how wet it is. ENERGY STAR's sizing table gives a portable-unit starting range of 20–30 pints per day for a slightly or moderately damp space under 2,000 square feet, 25–40 pints for a very damp space, and 30–50 pints for a wet space. Larger spaces use the corresponding larger ranges.

Those figures are not a universal guarantee. Room connections, temperature, ventilation, moisture sources, and the product's current rating all affect performance. Use the manufacturer's current sizing information before buying.

## Set a meaningful humidity target

EPA guidance recommends keeping indoor relative humidity below 60%, ideally between 30% and 50%, to help control moisture and mold. A small hygrometer gives you a better basis for adjustment than comfort alone.

If humidity remains high, look for leaks, condensation, wet materials, inadequate exhaust, or outdoor water entry. A dehumidifier removes moisture from the air; it does not repair the source.

ENERGY STAR publishes these ranges as sizing guidance. Product ratings, test conditions, and local availability can change, so compare the label and manual for the specific model.

## A practical selection path

### 1. Measure the area the unit must serve

Measure the room or area, then note whether doors normally stay open to other rooms. Do not assume that a unit rated for one closed room will control an entire floor.

### 2. Classify the moisture condition

Use a hygrometer and observe the room. Slightly damp means it feels humid but has no visible moisture; very damp or wet conditions include persistent condensation, damp spots, or water-related damage. If there is active leaking, flooding, or repeated water entry, repair the source and seek appropriate professional help.

### 3. Choose the capacity range, not an invented exact number

Use the table as a starting range, then compare the manufacturer's current capacity guidance. A larger unit is not a substitute for correcting building moisture, and a small unit may run continuously without controlling a connected area.

### 4. Check operation and drainage

Confirm that the unit can operate in the room's temperature range, has an automatic shutoff when the bucket is full, and supports the drainage method you can safely use. Follow the manual for clearance, filter cleaning, and electrical requirements.

### 5. Recheck the result

After the unit has operated under normal conditions, measure relative humidity again. If the space remains above the target or shows new dampness, investigate the moisture source rather than continually lowering the setting or buying another appliance.

## Common mistakes

- **Sizing by square footage alone:** Moisture condition is part of ENERGY STAR's sizing guidance.
- **Treating a dehumidifier as a leak repair:** Persistent water intrusion needs source correction.
- **Using comfort as the only measurement:** A hygrometer helps reveal humidity that does not feel obvious.
- **Ignoring the manual's operating limits:** Temperature, drainage, clearance, and filter requirements vary by model.

## FAQs

### Is a bigger dehumidifier always better?

No. Capacity must match the space and moisture condition, and performance depends on operating conditions. A larger unit cannot solve a leak or other continuing water source.

### What humidity should I aim for?

EPA guidance says below 60% and ideally 30–50% for moisture and mold control. The appropriate setting can depend on the building and conditions; avoid presenting one number as a guarantee of comfort or mold prevention.

### When should I consider a whole-home unit or professional assessment?

Consider whole-home guidance when several connected spaces remain humid or when a portable unit cannot maintain the target. Get professional help for active leaks, flooding, structural moisture, or recurring dampness whose source is not clear.
