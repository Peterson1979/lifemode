## The short answer

Press the test button on each smoke alarm once a month. If the alarm uses a replaceable battery, change it according to the alarm's instructions; if it has a sealed battery, replace the alarm when the unit reaches the end of its service life. Replace the whole alarm at 10 years from its manufacture date, or earlier if it does not pass a test or the manufacturer says to do so. Hardwired replacement or installation should be handled by a qualified electrician.

This routine is about keeping the warning system operational. A test button checks the alarm's warning function; it does not replace the manufacturer's maintenance instructions.

## What you need

- A step stool that is stable on the floor, if the alarm is out of reach.
- The alarm's manual or model instructions.
- The correct replacement battery, only if the alarm is designed for one.
- A phone or paper reminder for the monthly test.

## Five-step check

### 1. Find the manufacture date and power type

Look for the manufacture date on the alarm and note whether it uses a replaceable battery, a sealed battery, or household wiring with battery backup. Read the model instructions before opening or removing anything.

If the date is missing, the alarm is damaged, or you cannot determine how it is powered, treat that as a reason to consult the manufacturer or a qualified professional rather than guessing.

### 2. Test the alarm monthly

Press and hold the test button as directed by the manufacturer. The alarm should produce its test signal. Test every alarm, not just the one nearest the kitchen.

The U.S. Fire Administration recommends smoke alarms in every bedroom, outside sleeping areas, and on every level of the home, including the basement. Follow the product instructions and local requirements for exact placement.

### 3. Replace a battery only when the unit allows it

For a replaceable-battery alarm, install the battery type and orientation specified in the instructions, then test the alarm again. For a sealed-battery alarm, do not pry open the case or substitute a loose battery; replace the alarm as a unit when required.

Do not remove a battery to stop nuisance alarms. Use the alarm's hush feature and address the source of the nuisance according to the instructions. If the alarm continues to sound without an apparent cause, contact the manufacturer or a qualified professional.

### 4. Replace the whole alarm at 10 years

Replace the alarm at 10 years from its manufacture date, even if it still sounds during a test. Replace it sooner if it fails the test, is damaged, or the manufacturer specifies an earlier replacement.

### 5. Treat hardwired work as electrical work

If the alarm is hardwired, use a qualified electrician for installation or replacement. Do not disconnect household wiring or improvise a connection to make a replacement fit. After professional work, test the installed alarm and follow its instructions for battery backup and interconnection.

## Placement and cooking-related nuisance alarms

Smoke alarms should be installed inside bedrooms, outside sleeping areas, and on every level. The USFA pictograph guidance also places alarms at least 10 feet from a cooking appliance where possible; use the manufacturer's instructions and local code for the final placement. If cooking causes nuisance alarms, use the hush function and improve ventilation rather than disabling the alarm.

## Common mistakes

- **Testing only one alarm:** A home can have several alarms with different batteries, ages, or faults. Test each unit monthly.
- **Replacing only the battery in an old alarm:** A new battery does not reset the alarm's 10-year replacement interval.
- **Removing the battery when an alarm chirps:** This leaves the home without the intended warning. Follow the manual's troubleshooting steps or replace the unit when appropriate.
- **Attempting hardwired work without qualification:** Wiring errors can create an electrical hazard and can leave interconnected alarms unreliable.

## FAQs

### Does a test button prove that the alarm will detect every fire?

No. It confirms the alarm's test function. Keep the alarm installed in the required locations, maintain it as directed, and follow the manufacturer's instructions for the specific model.

### What should I do if the alarm does not sound during the test?

Check the manual for the correct test procedure and battery requirements. If the alarm still fails, replace it or have the hardwired unit assessed by a qualified professional. Do not rely on a failed alarm.

### How often should a household test smoke alarms?

The USFA guidance used here recommends testing monthly. Record the test date so that a missed check is easy to notice.
