# LifeMode Article Acceptance Checklist

Use this checklist for every LifeMode practical article. Mark each applicable item, record evidence for consequential claims, and give the article one disposition: **approved**, **revise**, **rejected**, or **held for evidence**.

## Automatic rejection or return-for-revision gates

Reject or return the article if any of these is true:

- [ ] It does not answer the stated reader question or decision.
- [ ] A material claim is unsupported, unverifiable, or contradicted by its cited source.
- [ ] It contains a fabricated citation, URL, quotation, statistic, credential, test, experience, or authority.
- [ ] It omits a known hazard, condition, limitation, exception, or professional boundary that could change the outcome.
- [ ] The headline or summary materially overpromises.
- [ ] The draft is repetitive, padded, keyword-stuffed, promotional, or a near-duplicate without a distinct reader purpose.
- [ ] Required metadata is missing, misleading, or incompatible with the active schema.
- [ ] A related link or route needed for reader use is broken.
- [ ] The editor cannot determine what was verified, what remains uncertain, or who reviewed it.

## 1. Reader problem and intent

- [ ] One concrete reader question, task, or decision is written in the brief.
- [ ] Intended reader, desired outcome, and likely consequence of error are explicit.
- [ ] Article type is appropriate: reference, protocol, decision, comparison, explainer, or checklist.
- [ ] Existing LifeMode overlap was checked; any difference in question, audience, or decision is stated.
- [ ] The title names the actual object/problem and outcome without misleading certainty.
- [ ] The opening gives a direct answer before general background.
- [ ] The quick summary is specific, accurate, and useful without the rest of the article.

## 2. Completeness and practical use

- [ ] The reader can identify the starting condition and the normal path.
- [ ] Materials, tools, quantities, units, durations, temperatures, sequence, and expected result are present where relevant.
- [ ] Every procedural step has the needed action, condition, warning, exception, or stop condition.
- [ ] A decision article defines criteria before recommending an option.
- [ ] Trade-offs, best-fit conditions, and disqualifying constraints are visible.
- [ ] Common mistakes describe real failure modes and how to correct them.
- [ ] FAQs answer genuine non-repetitive follow-up questions.
- [ ] The article is no longer than necessary for complete, safe coverage.
- [ ] Mechanism/explanation is included only where it improves correct action or decision quality.

## 3. Evidence and source verification

- [ ] A claim-to-source record exists for every consequential factual claim.
- [ ] Source authority matches the risk: official/primary/standards guidance for food, health, fire, chemical, electrical, structural, and legal claims; current manufacturer guidance for product operation.
- [ ] Every source was opened and its title, publisher, URL/document identifier, and date/version were recorded where available.
- [ ] Each citation supports the exact wording, scope, number, and condition used in the article.
- [ ] Numbers, thresholds, dates, temperatures, durations, technical terms, and compatibility claims were independently checked.
- [ ] Conflicting or limited evidence is disclosed rather than hidden.
- [ ] Unverified facts were removed, narrowed, clearly qualified, or held for evidence.
- [ ] A source list is not being treated as proof of verification.

## 4. Safety and professional boundaries

- [ ] Fire, food, chemical, health, electrical, structural, and other consequential risks have proportionate visible warnings.
- [ ] The article does not instruct the reader to defeat a safety device or disregard a manufacturer instruction.
- [ ] Stop conditions and professional/official hand-offs are explicit.
- [ ] The wording does not promise safety, medical/legal certainty, or universal results beyond the evidence.
- [ ] Units, concentrations, temperatures, and timing are safe and consistent.
- [ ] The article distinguishes official guidance from household practice and editorial preference.

## 5. Language, originality, and editorial integrity

- [ ] Headings help the reader find actions, criteria, mechanisms, exceptions, or limits.
- [ ] No generic scene-setting introduction could be pasted onto any topic.
- [ ] No paragraph repeats an earlier point without adding a condition or new reasoning.
- [ ] No keyword-stuffed headings or unnatural repetition appear.
- [ ] No unsupported superlatives, “experts say” claims, fake authority, fictional experience, or invented credentials appear.
- [ ] The article is independent guidance, not affiliate or sponsor copy in disguise.
- [ ] Any AI assistance was limited to permitted organization, drafting support, grammar, or formatting and was fully human-reviewed.
- [ ] The article does not claim to use or reproduce an unrecovered historical EverydayGuide prompt.

## 6. Metadata and technical integrity

- [ ] Required active-schema fields are present and valid.
- [ ] `contentType`, category, format, intent, risk level, and structured fields match the actual article.
- [ ] Title, description, summary, canonical/route data, and visible metadata agree.
- [ ] Author and reviewer identities/roles are truthful and do not imply unsupported credentials.
- [ ] Publication and update dates are accurate; a future update trigger is recorded for changing guidance.
- [ ] Sources and related content identify real destinations.
- [ ] Every internal link resolves; no missing related guide/tool/checklist is silently included.
- [ ] Build and relevant automated checks pass.
- [ ] Rendering success is recorded separately from factual/editorial approval.
